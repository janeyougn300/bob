# Micro-Drama Pitch Data

Numbers in a pitch deck are load-bearing. An executive who catches one stale or unsourced figure discounts every other claim in the document.

> **LIBRARY LAST VERIFIED: 20 August 2026.**
> Check today's date against that line before you cite anything below. More than one
> quarter past it, the library is a starting point for a search, not a source. Say so in
> the reply rather than citing it silently, and update the stamp when you refresh.

Two rules govern everything here:

1. **Search before citing.** This market moves in weeks. Every figure below carries a date. If today is more than roughly one quarter past that date, run a web search and update it before it goes in a deck. Never present a number from memory as current.
2. **Label the tier.** Reported figures and analyst estimates are different things and executives can tell. Mark estimates as estimates on the slide itself. Credibility gained by labeling exceeds credibility lost by admitting uncertainty.

**Why the stamp exists.** A dated figure in a persistent file gets trusted over a live search, because it is right there and it looks sourced. Every figure in this library was accurate when written and several have since been overtaken. The stamp is what stops a stale number reaching a slide with a confident footnote under it.

## Source tiers

**Tier 1, reported:** Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Media Partners Asia, company filings, Variety, TechCrunch, TIME, Deloitte.
**Tier 2, analyst estimate:** industry write-ups and consultancies quoting ranges without a named methodology. CPI, paywall conversion, and ARPPU almost always fall here.
**Tier 3, anecdotal:** practitioner blogs, forum reports. Use for texture, never for a number on a slide.

## Benchmark library

All figures dated. Verify before use.

### Category size

- 2025 full year: **$2.98B in-app revenue, up 115% year over year** (Sensor Tower, via Marketing Dive). Third-highest revenue growth of any app category that year.
- Q1 2025 global IAP: **≈$700M, roughly 4x Q1 2024** (Sensor Tower).
- Q1 2026 global IAP: **≈$750M, up about 20% year on year** (Sensor Tower, State of Short Drama Apps 2026).
- H1 2026, overseas apps specifically: **≈$1.27B IAP, up about 13% year on year** (36Kr, H1 2026). Overseas MAU projected to reach **389M by end of 2026**, penetration 20.9%.
- Deloitte 2026 TMT Predictions forecast category in-app revenue at **$3.8B in 2025 more than doubling to $7.8B in 2026**. Projection, not a result, and see the deceleration note below before citing it.
- Broader industry measure, including non-IAP revenue: **$11B in 2025, projected $14B by end of 2026**. Not comparable to the IAP figures above. Never mix the two on one slide.
- US share: **≈60% of global short-drama mobile revenue in 2024** (Sensor Tower). Deloitte projects the US at half of global in 2025 declining to **40%** as other markets convert.

> ### The deceleration, and why it matters in a room
>
> Growth has slowed hard and the library above shows it plainly: **+115% across 2025,
> +20% in Q1 2026, +13% in H1 2026 overseas.** The category is still growing and still
> large. It is no longer tripling.
>
> This puts Deloitte's $7.8B 2026 projection in tension with the actual run rate. Four
> quarters at roughly the Q1 2026 level lands near $3B on the IAP measure, not $7.8B.
> The projection was made against 2025's growth curve and the curve bent.
>
> A deck that cites the $7.8B doubling without acknowledging the run rate is exposed to
> exactly one question, and it is the question a numerate executive asks first. Two
> honest ways to handle it. Cite the actual trailing figures and argue the category is
> maturing into a durable business rather than a bubble, which is the stronger argument
> anyway. Or cite the projection and name the deceleration in the same breath, with the
> reason you still believe the direction.
>
> Do not cite the projection alone. Deceleration is not a reason to avoid the category,
> and pretending it is not happening is what loses the room.

### Platform scale

- **ReelShort** (Crazy Maple Studio / COL Group): **≈$1.2B gross consumer spending across 2025, up 119%** (Appfigures via TechCrunch, 23 Jan 2026). **≈$140M IAP in Q1 2026**, still the top-grossing short drama app (Sensor Tower). Crossed **50M MAU** in 2025. All-original English productions based in LA. Still loss-making.
- **DramaBox** (Dianzhong): **$276M gross consumer spending across 2025** (Appfigures via TechCrunch, Jan 2026). Separately **$323M revenue and $10M net profit in 2024** (Media Partners Asia via Variety, Sept 2025), profitable, unlike ReelShort. Also crossed **50M MAU** in 2025.
- Q1 2025 head to head: ReelShort **$130M** (+31%), DramaBox **$120M** (+29%) IAP (Sensor Tower).
- ReelShort 2024 scale: **≈$400M, unprofitable** (MPA via Variety).
- The two together held **≈70% of global short-drama IAP in Q1 2025**.

**Two measurement traps in the numbers above.** Both catch people.

*Gross consumer spending is not revenue.* It is charged before the store's cut. ReelShort's $1.2B and DramaBox's $276M are that measure. DramaBox at $323M (MPA, revenue) versus $276M (Appfigures, gross consumer spend) are different measures across different periods, not a contradiction.

*Quarterly IAP does not annualize to gross consumer spend.* ReelShort at $140M in Q1 2026 is not on a $1.2B-equivalent pace, because the two figures count different things. Pick one measure per slide and footnote which.

### Unit economics (Tier 2, label as estimates on the slide)

- **US CPI: ≈$3–$7**, skewing above $5 on iOS.
- **Free-to-paid / paywall conversion: ≈3–8%.** Widened from the 3–5% previously carried here; 2026 sources put the top of the range higher.
- **Monthly ARPPU: ≈$15–$35.** All-user ARPU $0.50–$2.50.
- **Whale concentration:** top ~10% of payers drive 40–60% of revenue. A minority of payers funds the entire model, which is why paywall placement outranks almost every other lever.
- **Creative volume is the real UA cost.** ReelShort has reportedly deployed **1.34 million creatives** globally. If a deck models user acquisition without a creative production line item, that is the hole a platform executive will find.

### Production and licensing (Tier 1 unless marked)

- **Current production band: $150K–$300K** for a US vertical series, with the trade-press median at **$150K–$200K** (2026).
- Full range by tier: **$12K** (China minimum) to **$300K** (US studio standard), reaching **$400K–$600K** for US premium. The **$100K floor** is set by AI-assisted indie production. The **$300K ceiling** is effectively set by SAG-AFTRA's Verticals Agreement.
- Historic comparison: a 60–90 episode ReelShort series cost **≈$300K on average** (TIME, 2024). The band has widened downward since, not upward.
- Both major platforms **buy outright at $150K–$250K per 60–90 episode series**; producer fees run **10–15%** (Filmustage). One producer has publicly disclosed targeting a **$1M-plus** licensing sale to a major platform, which is an outlier and should be labelled as one if cited.
- **Two different ROI numbers circulate and they are not in conflict.** Recoup threshold for an original US production is **1.5–2.0** (Real Reel, 2025). A *hit* returns **5x–20x** the production budget (2026). Use the first for a base case and the second only when explicitly modelling upside. Presenting 5x–20x as an expected return is the fastest way to lose a numerate room.

## Trope taxonomy

Vertical buyers read trope tags before they read prose. Tag every title.

**Romance:** fated mates, arranged marriage, fake marriage, contract marriage, second-chance romance, age gap, forbidden romance, marriage of convenience, enemies to lovers.

**Power fantasy:** secret billionaire, hidden heir, secret identity reveal, disguised CEO, returning war hero, underestimated protagonist, glow-up.

**Revenge:** betrayal and return, revenge marriage, family usurpation, wrongful imprisonment, faked death return.

**Family ethics:** swapped at birth, custody fight, mother-in-law conflict, inheritance dispute, prodigal return.

**Supernatural crossovers:** werewolf/alpha, vampire, reincarnation, time loop, body swap, system/transmigration.

### Genre as emotion, not label

Vertical buyers evaluate whether a title reliably delivers **Shock, Hurt, or Release**, mapped across the four core genres (Romance, Revenge, Power Fantasy, Family Ethics). Pitch the promised emotional experience, not the category. A slide saying "Romance" says nothing. A slide saying "the humiliation-then-vindication cycle, three times per ten episodes" says something a buyer can model.

## Comps framework

Never comp on premise alone. Map each title on two axes:

1. **Emotion delivered** (Shock / Hurt / Release)
2. **Trope cluster**

Then name platform titles that occupy the same cell and performed. A comp is an argument that demand has been demonstrated, not a statement that two shows are similar.

Strongest comp slide format: three comps, each with the title, the platform, one performance fact, and one line on what our title does differently. Comps that are only aspirational ("our *Succession*") read as naive to this buyer.

Prestige comps (HBO/Showtime titles) serve a different purpose: they establish tone and production ambition, not demand. Keep the two comp types on separate rows and label them.

## What a vertical pitch packet contains

Reverse-engineered from platform competitions and buyer statements. Shorter and tighter than a horizontal TV pitch:

1. **Logline naming the tropes.** No literary indirection.
2. **One-paragraph premise naming the structural conflict.** "She's the fake bride sold to the wrong brother," not "a story about family and identity."
3. **Four to six character cards.** Male lead, female lead, second-lead antagonist, one or two supporting. More than six loses the platform.
4. **A 60–100 episode arc map with the paywall episode explicitly flagged.**
5. **First ~10 episodes scripted.**
6. **Hook and cliffhanger logic.**
7. **Production envelope**, tied to SAG-AFTRA vertical agreements where relevant.
8. **Comps.**

If a deck is missing the paywall flag or the character card count is over six, flag it before delivery.

## Economics slide template

One slide, four numbers, no paragraph:

| Line | Source of figure |
|---|---|
| Production cost per series | Our budget |
| Licensing / outright range | $150K–$250K (Tier 1) |
| Target ROI | 1.5–2.0 (Tier 1) |
| Payback window | Our model |

Footnote every external figure with source and date in 9pt grey. This footnote is not optional; it is the thing that makes the slide believable.

## Greenlight rationale pattern

Three blocks, three distinct angles. Never three phrasings of one thought.

Draw from: **format fit** (does the story produce a hook every 60–90 seconds), **demonstrated demand** (comps with performance data), **production feasibility** (locations that work vertical, cast size, shoot days), **slate balance** (what this title covers that others don't), **IP position** (what we already own).

Then pressure-test. Name the three strongest objections an executive could raise, and rewrite so each rationale block answers its objection before it gets asked. Deliver the objections to the user alongside the rationale so they are not surprised in the room.

## Presenting uncertainty

- Put the date next to any figure older than one quarter.
- Mark Tier 2 figures as "industry estimate" inline.
- When two credible sources conflict, show both with their measures named, or pick one and footnote why.
- Never invent a figure to fill a slide. An empty cell labeled "not disclosed" is stronger than a fabricated number, and far stronger than a number an executive can disprove.
