HOW TO FILL THE COLUMNS

SIGN. Plus or minus, as an answer to the season question. Not whether the episode was nice.
OPEN VALUE and CLOSE VALUE. One word each. Trusted, suspect, wanted, disposable, safe, owned, protected, exposed. Not a summary of events.
TURN. Name the room and the witnesses. A flip nobody saw is private bad luck.
EXIT FACT. Who someone is, what someone owns, who signed, who is alive. Name the plan it invalidates.
WRONG ANSWER. Write the specific answer the viewer will jump to. If you cannot write one, the exit is a blackout. If the answer you write is correct, the exit is a reveal and the pull is gone.

AUDIT PASS. Run this after the grid is full and before anyone drafts.

1. Any row where OPEN VALUE equals CLOSE VALUE gets merged into its neighbour. No exceptions.
2. Read the SIGN column top to bottom. Two identical signs adjacent are allowed only where the second is a different kind of bad, never a larger amount of the same bad. Mark every violation and reorder.
3. Every episode's first three seconds: name the want, or show the taking, or open mid-reaction. Write which one in the margin. If more than four consecutive episodes use the same opening shape, rebuild them.
4. Count the episodes where the lead is waiting, absorbing, or reacting with no urgent want of her own. Each of those gets reassigned to whoever's want makes the episode happen.
5. Count the episodes where none of the three opponents applies pressure. Any run of three or more is a pacing hole.
6. Confirm the gate episode does not sit on a discharge. If the gate is an explosion, a rescue, or an answer, move it.
7. Confirm the poster is spent. If eps 1 to 8 are arrival, setup and paperwork, the viewer is being asked to pay to start the show.
8. Confirm episode 13 changes the lead's standing more than episode 11 did, and less than episode 12 did.
