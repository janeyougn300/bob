# Micro-Drama Pitch Data

Numbers in a pitch deck are load-bearing. An executive who catches one stale or unsourced figure discounts every other claim in the document.

Two rules govern everything here:

1. **Search before citing.** This market moves in weeks. Every figure below carries a date. If today is more than roughly one quarter past that date, run a web search and update it before it goes in a deck. Never present a number from memory as current.
2. **Label the tier.** Reported figures and analyst estimates are different things and executives can tell. Mark estimates as estimates on the slide itself. Credibility gained by labeling exceeds credibility lost by admitting uncertainty.

## Source tiers

**Tier 1, reported:** Sensor Tower, Appfigures, Media Partners Asia, company filings, Variety, TechCrunch, TIME, Deloitte.
**Tier 2, analyst estimate:** industry write-ups and consultancies quoting ranges without a named methodology. CPI, paywall conversion, and ARPPU almost always fall here.
**Tier 3, anecdotal:** practitioner blogs, forum reports. Use for texture, never for a number on a slide.

## Benchmark library

All figures dated. Verify before use.

### Category size

- Short-drama apps generated **$2.98B in in-app revenue across 2025, up 115% year over year** (Sensor Tower, via Marketing Dive). Third-highest revenue growth of any app category that year.
- Q1 2025 global in-app purchase revenue: **≈$700M, roughly 4x Q1 2024** (Sensor Tower).
- Deloitte 2026 TMT Predictions forecast category in-app revenue at **$3.8B in 2025, more than doubling to $7.8B in 2026**. This is a projection, not a result.
- US share: **≈60% of global short-drama mobile revenue in 2024** (Sensor Tower). Deloitte projects the US at half of global revenue in 2025 declining to **40%** as other markets convert.

### Platform scale

- **ReelShort** (Crazy Maple Studio / COL Group): **≈$1.2B gross consumer spending in 2025, up 119% from 2024** (Appfigures, via TechCrunch, 23 Jan 2026). 70M+ MAU. All-original English productions based in LA. Still loss-making.
- **DramaBox** (Dianzhong): **$276M gross consumer spending in 2025** (Appfigures via TechCrunch, Jan 2026). Separately, **$323M revenue and $10M net profit in 2024** (Media Partners Asia, via Variety, Sept 2025) — profitable, unlike ReelShort.
- ReelShort 2024 scale: **≈$400M, unprofitable** (MPA via Variety).
- The two platforms together held **≈70% of global short-drama IAP in Q1 2025**.

**Note the apparent conflict:** DramaBox at $323M (MPA, revenue) versus $276M (Appfigures, gross consumer spend) are different measures across different periods, not a contradiction. If both appear in a deck, footnote the distinction or use only one.

### Unit economics (Tier 2 — label as estimates)

- **US CPI: ≈$3–$7**, skewing above $5 on iOS.
- **Free-to-paid / paywall conversion: ≈3–5%.**
- **Monthly ARPPU: ≈$15–$35.** All-user ARPU $0.50–$2.50.
- **Whale concentration:** top ~10% of payers drive 40–60% of revenue.

### Production and licensing (Tier 1)

- Both major platforms **buy outright at $150K–$250K per 60–90 episode series**; producer fees run **10–15%** (Filmustage).
- A 60–90 episode ReelShort series costs **≈$300K on average** to produce (TIME, 2024).
- Original US productions typically need **ROI of 1.5–2.0** to recoup (Real Reel, 2025).

## Trope taxonomy

Vertical buyers read trope tags before they read prose. Tag every title.

**Romance:** fated mates, arranged marriage, fake marriage, contract marriage, second-chance romance, age gap, forbidden romance, marriage of convenience, enemies to lovers.

**Power fantasy:** secret billionaire, hidden heir, secret identity reveal, disguised CEO, returning war hero, underestimated protagonist, glow-up.

**Revenge:** betrayal and return, revenge marriage, family usurpation, wrongful imprisonment, faked death return.

**Family ethics:** swapped at birth, custody fight, mother-in-law conflict, inheritance dispute, prodigal return.

**Supernatural crossovers:** werewolf/alpha, vampire, reincarnation, time loop, body swap, system/transmigration.

### Genre as emotion, not label

Vertical buyers evaluate whether a title reliably delivers **Shock, Hurt, or Release**, mapped across the four core genres (Romance, Revenge, Power Fantasy, Family Ethics). Pitch the promised emotional experience, not the category. A slide saying "Romance" says nothing. A slide saying "the humiliation-then-vindication cycle, three times per ten episodes" says something a buyer can model.

## Comps framework

Never comp on premise alone. Map each title on two axes:

1. **Emotion delivered** (Shock / Hurt / Release)
2. **Trope cluster**

Then name platform titles that occupy the same cell and performed. A comp is an argument that demand has been demonstrated, not a statement that two shows are similar.

Strongest comp slide format: three comps, each with the title, the platform, one performance fact, and one line on what our title does differently. Comps that are only aspirational ("our *Succession*") read as naive to this buyer.

Prestige comps (HBO/Showtime titles) serve a different purpose: they establish tone and production ambition, not demand. Keep the two comp types on separate rows and label them.

## What a vertical pitch packet contains

Reverse-engineered from platform competitions and buyer statements. Shorter and tighter than a horizontal TV pitch:

1. **Logline naming the tropes.** No literary indirection.
2. **One-paragraph premise naming the structural conflict.** "She's the fake bride sold to the wrong brother," not "a story about family and identity."
3. **Four to six character cards.** Male lead, female lead, second-lead antagonist, one or two supporting. More than six loses the platform.
4. **A 60–100 episode arc map with the paywall episode explicitly flagged.**
5. **First ~10 episodes scripted.**
6. **Hook and cliffhanger logic.**
7. **Production envelope**, tied to SAG-AFTRA vertical agreements where relevant.
8. **Comps.**

If a deck is missing the paywall flag or the character card count is over six, flag it before delivery.

## Economics slide template

One slide, four numbers, no paragraph:

| Line | Source of figure |
|---|---|
| Production cost per series | Our budget |
| Licensing / outright range | $150K–$250K (Tier 1) |
| Target ROI | 1.5–2.0 (Tier 1) |
| Payback window | Our model |

Footnote every external figure with source and date in 9pt grey. This footnote is not optional; it is the thing that makes the slide believable.

## Greenlight rationale pattern

Three blocks, three distinct angles. Never three phrasings of one thought.

Draw from: **format fit** (does the story produce a hook every 60–90 seconds), **demonstrated demand** (comps with performance data), **production feasibility** (locations that work vertical, cast size, shoot days), **slate balance** (what this title covers that others don't), **IP position** (what we already own).

Then pressure-test. Name the three strongest objections an executive could raise, and rewrite so each rationale block answers its objection before it gets asked. Deliver the objections to the user alongside the rationale so they are not surprised in the room.

## Presenting uncertainty

- Put the date next to any figure older than one quarter.
- Mark Tier 2 figures as "industry estimate" inline.
- When two credible sources conflict, show both with their measures named, or pick one and footnote why.
- Never invent a figure to fill a slide. An empty cell labeled "not disclosed" is stronger than a fabricated number, and far stronger than a number an executive can disprove.
