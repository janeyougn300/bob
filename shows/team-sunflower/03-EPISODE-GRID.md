# EPISODE GRID: TEAM SUNFLOWER

Fill every cell before a single line of script is written. A blank cell is a hole in the
season, not a decision you can make later.

**The one ban all three shapes share: never gate on a discharge.** Not the explosion,
not the rescue, not the answer. If you gate on the explosion the tension is spent and
the first paid episode opens on aftermath.

## The grid

- **SIGN.** `+` or `-` as an answer to the season question. Never two of the same
  back to back. One exemption: a second same-sign exit is allowed when it is a
  *different kind* of bad, never a larger amount of the same bad.
- **OPEN / CLOSE VALUE.** One word each. Trusted to suspect. Wanted to disposable.
  Safe to owned. **If the two words match, merge this episode into its neighbour.**
- **TURN.** Who outranks whom at the top, who outranks whom by the end, and who
  watched. Without witnesses the fall reads as private bad luck.
- **EXIT FACT.** The new fact that cancels the hero's plan. One line or one image.
- **WRONG ANSWER LEFT AVAILABLE.** The viewer must be able to name a specific next
  answer, and it must be available and wrong. If the fact settles the question it is a
  reveal and the pull ends. If it names nothing it is a blackout and confusion has no
  pull at all.
- **HOOK TYPE.** Revelation / Threat / Mystery / Character revelation / Tactical /
  Foreboding. Never two the same back to back.

## Subtraction ledger

For every chase, fight, heist, capture, gate or parallel thread: name the one resource
the protagonist no longer has, or the one thing the antagonist can no longer do, at the
moment it closes. Three words. Visible in frame. Acknowledged later in one line.

**If that sentence cannot be written, cut the sequence or fold it into another one.**
A set piece that returns the protagonist to their starting inventory is filler no matter
how well it is staged.

## Clip list

Every few episodes needs one beat sharp enough to cut into a standalone ad with no
context. Flag these as they are written, never in a pass at the end.

What clips: single close-ups with high physical specificity. Hands doing something to a
body. A slow lean-in that stops before contact. A face doing something involuntary
before it gets covered. A look one character does not see but the audience does. A line
delivered in complete stillness.
